Our Storied History

Chapter 1: The "New Fun Slides"

The Republic's story begins on October 15, 2025. Aidyn Kerns created "The New Fun Slides" as a response to other chaotic Google Slideshows. The goal was to create a zone where friends could share content and laugh without fear of their work being deleted or the slides lagging out. Though the initial rules were loose, the slideshow quickly gained popularity, establishing a core group of "higher-ups" including Jack Crawford, Wyatt Belknap, Zach Rose, and Zach Becher.

Chapter 2: The Birth of "Spungbung" & The Republic

A pivotal moment occurred on October 16, 2025, when Wyatt Belknap and Zachary Ludwig created a unique piece of art. Wyatt named this creation "Spungbung," which inspired the core "Spung" ideology and the "Spung Spangled Banner." After a user, Zachary Ludwig, was removed for inappropriate content and then re-added (suspected by Zachary Becher), he deleted all the slides. Aidyn Kerns restored the slides, leading to a "mass removal" and the formal creation of "The Republic of Spung" to establish order. Aidyn Kerns became the first president without an election, and the first Charter of Governance was drafted by Jack Crawford, establishing the positions of Executive Ruler and Executor, won by Jack Crawford and Zachary Rose, respectively.

Chapter 3: The Trial of Zachary Bechar

The first official trial in the Republic's history was against Zachary Bechar, who was charged with treason for re-adding Zachary Ludwig after his removal. He confessed to the charge and, by a unanimous decision, was removed from the slides, setting a precedent for the rule of law.

Chapter 4: The First Chinchillian War

This event, which took place in Ms. Masl's room on November 17, 2025, escalated from a verbal disagreement between Zachary Rose and Zachary Ludwig into a **brief physical altercation**. Following a minor accidental contact, the conflict escalated when Mr. Ludwig used an ***Animal Farm* book in an aggressive manner** against Mr. Rose. The situation involved **minor book throwings and the falling of chairs**, requiring the immediate intervention of others present, including Jackson Claeyssen, Jack Crawford, and Wyatt Belknap, before Ms. Masl restored order. This intense, yet swiftly resolved, incident highlighted significant political tensions preceding the day's election.

Chapter 5: The First General Election (Nov 17, 2025)

The first scheduled General Election was held on November 17, 2025, marking a peaceful transfer of power and solidifying the Republic's democratic processes. The citizens voted for a new administration and on key issues.

Election Results:
  • Presidential: Wyatt Belknap (Spatian) defeated Landon Boyle (Traditional) with a decisive 12-1 vote.
  • Senator-Executive Ruler: Jack Crawford (Spatian) was elected with a 13-0 vote.
  • Senator-Executioner: Zachary Rose (Traditional) was elected, defeating Aidyn Kerns (Spatian) 11-2.
  • Colony Name: The name "New Spungia" was chosen for the new colony with 5 votes, solidifying the new name of the nation's first colonu.
  • Issue 1 (Money shares and businesses): Passed with an 8-5 vote, scheduled to be put in place on November 20, 2025.

Charter of Governance

Key Principles

  • Digital Integrity is Mandatory: The deletion, alteration, or unauthorized modification of official rule sets, slides, or governmental statutes is strictly prohibited (Section 1.1.1).
  • Content Regulation: A limit of five (5) animated GIFs is imposed per slide to prevent digital spam and lag (Section 1.1.3).
  • Executive Power: The President serves a one-month term and holds the right to veto legislation (Section 4.1.1, 2.2.3).
  • Senate Authority: The Senate (Executive Ruler & Executor) is elected for one-month terms and manages the legislative and enforcement agendas (Section 2.1.1, 2.3.1).
  • Strict Penalties: A first infraction results in a three (3) day suspension; a third or subsequent infraction results in permanent and irrevocable revocation of access (Section 1.4.1, 1.4.2).

Part I: Principles of Conduct and Digital Integrity

Chapter 1: The Code of Conduct for Digital Spaces
Section 1.1: Upholding Content Integrity
  • 1.1.1: Any act of deletion, alteration, or unauthorized modification of the official rule sets, governmental statutes, or convened meeting presentation slides is hereby declared a severe offense and is strictly prohibited.
  • 1.1.2: Presentation slides specifically designated for the purpose of promoting or informing about "4simpleproblems (4SP)" are to be regarded as foundational content and shall not be subjected to deletion or alteration by any user without explicit authorization from the governing body.
  • 1.1.3: The dissemination and inclusion of animated Graphics Interchange Format (GIF) files on any given slide are hereby regulated. A maximum limit of five (5) such files is imposed per slide. Any excessive or unsolicited propagation of GIFs shall be classified as digital spam and addressed accordingly under established penalty clauses.
Section 1.2: Allocation and Sanctity of User Content Domains
  • 1.2.1: To foster individual expression and responsibility, each duly recognized user shall be allocated specific, dedicated presentation slides.
  • 1.2.2: These designated slides constitute the exclusive content domain of the assigned user. Such domains are inviolable and shall not be subject to editing, modification, or deletion by any other party, ensuring personal digital sovereignty within the platform.
Section 1.3: Procedures for User Onboarding and System Access
  • 1.3.1: Formal requests pertaining to the addition of new users to the Republic's digital platform or slide system shall be formally directed to designated administrators: Aidyn, Zach R, or Jack. These individuals are tasked with overseeing the onboarding process.
Section 1.4: Enforcement Mechanisms and Penalty Framework
  • 1.4.1: First Infraction: Any individual found to be in violation of the rules delineated within this chapter shall incur a mandatory penalty, consisting of a three (3) day suspension of all access and editing privileges pertaining to the presentation slides. This period is intended for reflection and corrective action.
  • 1.4.2: Subsequent Infractions: A third (3rd) or any subsequent infraction of these established rules by the same individual shall result in the permanent and irrevocable revocation of all access rights to the platform and its associated slide system. This measure ensures the integrity and stability of the Republic's digital infrastructure.
Section 1.5: Protocols for Official Communication and Meetings
  • 1.5.1: All official governmental and senatorial meetings, duly convened by the presiding Senators and Presidents, shall be formally announced and communicated through the designated meeting slides. This ensures consistent and authoritative dissemination of critical information.
Section 1.6: Restrictions on Prohibited Content
  • 1.6.1: The deletion, unauthorized modification, or the introduction of extraneous content that alters the integrity of any material pertaining to the official Spongebob episodes, when presented within the Republic's official digital spaces, is strictly prohibited.

Part II: The Framework of Republican Governance

Chapter 2: The Senate and Legislative Authority
Article I: Structure and Leadership of the Senate
  • Section 2.1.1: Senate Offices: The esteemed Senate of the Republic shall comprise two (2) principal leadership positions: the Executive Ruler and the Executor. These individuals shall guide the legislative agenda and decision-making processes.
  • Section 2.1.2: Decisive Voting Authority: The Executive Ruler and the Executor shall hold the authority to cast the decisive votes on all matters of Major Legislation brought forth and passed by the regular membership of the Senate, thereby ensuring clear direction and resolution.
Article II: Legislative Processes and Executive Prerogatives
  • Section 2.2.1: Definition of Major Legislation: For the comprehensive interpretation of this governing document, "Major Legislation" shall be defined as any proposed statute, decree, or law that consists of more than one (1) distinct part or component.
  • Section 2.2.2: Mechanism for Resolving Tie Votes: In the infrequent event that the Executive Ruler and the Executor are unable to achieve a unanimous consensus on any Major Legislation, a formal tie vote shall be declared. In such instances, the President of the Republic shall assume the role of final arbiter, casting the deciding vote to resolve the legislative impasse.
  • Section 2.2.3: The Presidential Veto Power: The President of the Republic retains the inherent and absolute right to veto any legislative act, irrespective of its classification as Minor or Major Legislation. A presidential veto, once exercised, shall be considered final and absolute, not subject to override by any legislative body or process.
Article III: Election and Tenure of Senators
  • Section 2.3.1: Senatorial Term: Senators shall be elected to serve a dedicated term of office lasting one (1) calendar month. This ensures regular engagement and representation.
  • Section 2.3.2: Schedule for Senatorial Elections: Regular elections for all Senatorial positions shall be conducted on a standardized monthly basis. The inaugural election to establish the first cohort of Senators shall be held on the seventeenth (17th) day of November.
Chapter 3: Executive Authorities and Their Mandates
Article IV: Powers and Limitations of the Executive Ruler
  • Section 3.4.1: Authority Over Minor Legislation: The Executive Ruler is hereby vested with the authority to promulgate and enact Minor Legislation. However, this legislative power is constrained, limited to the creation of no more than one (1) minor law per weekly cycle.
  • Section 3.4.2: Designation of Current Office Holder: Mr. Jack Crawford is hereby officially appointed and recognized as the Executive Ruler. He shall assume all powers and operate within the limitations prescribed by this document until the subsequent scheduled election for this office.
Article V: Powers and Responsibilities of the Executor
  • Section 3.5.1: Authority of Discretionary Removal: The Executor possesses the authority to remove any individual from participation within "The slides" (referring to the presentation platform). This authority is circumscribed by specific protected classes of persons, namely:
    • Individuals formally recognized and designated as Trusted Citizens.
    • Any individual who is officially undergoing the process of running for a Senate position.
    • Any individual who is currently serving as an active member of the Senate.
  • Section 3.5.2: Authority of Discretionary Modification: Beyond removal, the Executor shall also possess the authority to remove or meticulously edit the content of any "slide" belonging to a regular Citizen, subject to the broader provisions of this charter.
  • Section 3.5.3: Designation of Current Office Holder: Mr. Zachary Rose is hereby officially appointed and recognized as the Executor. He shall assume all powers and operate within the limitations prescribed by this document until the subsequent scheduled election for this office.
Chapter 4: The Presidency and Executive Powers
Article I: Presidential Term and Succession
  • Section 4.1.1: Duration of Presidential Term: The President of the Republic shall hold office for a fixed term of one (1) consecutive months. The subsequent election to fill the office of President shall be conducted on the seventeenth (17th) day of November.
  • Section 4.1.2: Identification of Incumbent: The current incumbent holding the office of President is Mr. Aidyn Kerns.
Article II: Presidential Legislative Powers and Operational Constraints
  • Section 4.2.1: Power to Initiate Legislation: The President of the Republic is formally authorized to propose, introduce, and champion new legislative initiatives.
  • Section 4.2.2: The Presidential Veto: The President holds the power to exercise a veto over any legislation duly passed by the legislative body. This significant power is subject to a strict limitation, capped at a maximum of three (3) vetoes per single presidential term.
  • Section 4.2.3: Resolution of Legislative Disputes: In the event of any dispute or significant disagreement arising between the President and the legislative body (comprising the senators), the final determination rendered by the President shall serve as the conclusive resolution, binding upon all parties.
  • Section 4.2.4: The Advisory Referendum Mechanism: Should the President find themselves undecided or seeking broader consensus on a particular piece of legislation, the matter may be formally referred to all citizens for a direct, non-binding vote. This process is commonly known as an advisory referendum.
  • Section 4.2.5: Stipulations for "Vascular Laws": Any proposed legislation formally classified as a "Vascular law" – defined herein as any set of four (4) or more laws presented concurrently for consideration – shall be subject to a dual vote. This vote requires the approval of both the President and the collective body of the two (2) Senators.

Part III: Status, Rights, and Judicial Frameworks

Chapter 5: Articles of Trust and Governance
Article I: The Status of Trusted Citizens
  • Section 5.1.1: Privileges Afforded to Trusted Citizens: Individuals formally recognized and designated as Trusted Citizens shall be granted exemption from any punitive or disciplinary actions that may be initiated by the Executive Authority. This exemption is contingent upon adherence to all other provisions outlined within this document.
  • Section 5.1.2: Limitations Imposed Upon Trusted Citizens: Notwithstanding their privileged status, Trusted Citizens shall not possess any voting rights within the legislative or executive processes of the Republic's governing bodies. Their role is primarily advisory and observational in these capacities.
  • Section 5.1.3: Application Protocol for Trusted Citizen Status: A formal, standardized application process shall be meticulously established for any individual seeking the esteemed designation of Trusted Citizen. All such applications must be formally submitted for thorough review and consideration.
  • Section 5.1.4: Joint Deliberation on Applications: The approval or disapproval of all applications for Trusted Citizen status shall be determined through a joint deliberation process. This process involves both the President and the Senate, who shall render their decision based on a majority vote.
  • Section 5.1.5: Revocation of Trusted Citizen Status: The esteemed status of Trusted Citizen, once granted, may be revoked. Such revocation requires a formal vote, necessitating a majority agreement from both the President and the Senate.
Article II: Procedures Governing Judicial Proceedings
  • Section 5.2.1: Expungement of Case Records: Any proposal seeking the formal expungement or the removal of an individual's specific case record, or any presentation made within a trial proceeding, shall be subject to a formal vote by the relevant judicial or governing body.
Chapter 6: Bylaws Governing Designated Citizens
Article III: Exemption from Executor Actions
  • Section 6.1.1: Protection from Arbitrary Sanctions: Individuals formally recognized as Designated Citizens shall be explicitly exempt from any and all punitive actions, threats, or undue influence that might be levied by the Executor of this body. The Executor is hereby explicitly precluded from imposing sanctions, liabilities, or making arbitrary determinations against such Designated Citizens.
Article IV: Restrictions on Participation in Governance
  • Section 6.2.1: Disenfranchisement from Political Processes: Designated Citizens are hereby formally disenfranchised. They are barred from participating in any electoral processes, casting legislative votes, or engaging in deliberative decision-making procedures within the jurisdiction of this Republic.
Article V: The Application and Adjudication Process for Designated Citizen Status
  • Section 6.3.1: Formal Application Requirements: A formal application protocol shall be meticulously established for individuals aspiring to achieve the designation of Designated Citizen. This protocol mandates the submission of all prescribed documentation and strict adherence to specified procedural requirements.
  • Section 6.3.2: Review and Final Adjudication: All applications submitted for Designated Citizen status shall undergo a comprehensive review and final adjudication. This process will be conducted by the collective authority comprising the President and the Senatorial Council. Their decision to approve or disapprove any such application shall be deemed final and irrevocably binding.
Article VI: Revocation of Designated Citizen Status
  • Section 6.4.1: Unilateral Revocation Clause: The status of Designated Citizen may be subject to unilateral revocation. Such a revocation shall necessitate a formal vote, requiring the agreement of both the President and the Senatorial Council.
Article VII: Procedural Guidelines for Formal Proceedings
  • Section 6.5.1: Validation of Submitted Materials: During the course of any formal trial or proceeding, a vote may be duly initiated for the specific purpose of removing, striking, or otherwise invalidating any submitted documentation, evidentiary presentations, or official records that pertain to a party involved in said proceeding.

Part IV: Electoral Frameworks and Legislative Enactment

Chapter 7: Bylaws Governing Elections and the Passage of Laws
Article I: Electoral Schedules and Prohibitions
  • Section 7.1.1: Senatorial Election Cadence: Elections for the esteemed position of Senator shall be conducted on a consistent monthly basis, ensuring continuous representation.
  • Section 7.1.2: Presidential Election Cadence: Elections for the office of President shall be conducted on a bi-monthly schedule, occurring every two months, to maintain executive continuity.
  • Section 7.1.3: Prohibition on Legislative Voting During Election Periods: To ensure the integrity of the electoral process, no legislative voting proceedings, regardless of the classification of the law under consideration, shall be permitted or conducted on any day officially designated for a scheduled election.
Article II: Classification, Process, and Enactment of Laws
  • Section 7.2.1: Promulgation of Minor Laws: The sole authority and final discretion for the promulgation and enactment of any legislation classified as a Minor Law shall rest with the Executive Ruler, acting as President.
  • Section 7.2.2: Process for Enacting Major Laws:
    • Legislation classified as a Major Law must first be formally submitted for the approval of both members of the Senatorial Council.
    • In the event that the Senatorial Council cannot reach a unanimous decision or consensus on a proposed Major Law, the matter shall subsequently be submitted to the President for a final determination.
    • Should the President be unable to render a definitive decision or remain undecided on a Major Law after its submission, the proposed legislation shall then be put forth for a direct vote by the general citizenry, commonly referred to as a popular referendum.
  • Section 7.2.3: Special Provisions for Vascular Laws:
    • Any legislation formally designated as a Vascular Law shall be subject to a vote by a specific quorum: the President and the two (2) Senators.
    • For a Vascular Law to be formally approved and enacted into law, a minimum of two (2) out of the three (3) aforementioned officials must cast affirmative votes.

  • 8.2.1: The appointment of the Council Assistant shall be determined through the collective, consensus-driven decision of the Council.

  • 8.3.1: The Council reserves the unequivocal right to revoke the title and authority vested in the Council Assistant. Part VI: Procedural Guidelines for Adjudication and Trials

Chapter 9: The Trial Procedures of the Republic
  • Section 9.1: The Senatorial Vote in Trials: In matters of adjudication, the senators shall convene to cast votes determining the innocence or guilt of a Trusted Citizen. Furthermore, they shall decide whether to uphold or delete the accused's presentation slide, and whether an individual should be formally removed from participation.
  • Section 9.2: Presidential Intervention in Unresolved Cases: In the event that the Senators are unable to reach a consensus on a verdict or decision, the President shall intervene. Should the President remain undecided after deliberation, the accused shall be declared innocent.
  • Section 9.3: Custodianship of Trial Records: The Council Assistant is tasked with the diligent and accurate record-keeping and tracking of all official trials conducted within the Republic.

Part X: The establishment of Colonies

Chapter 13: The establishment of Colonies
  • Article 13.1: The establishment of an official colony within the Republic of Spung necessitates the procurement and issuance of an official Republic of Spung Charter (RSC). Prior to the dispatch of any colonial contingent, the RSC must secure affirmative approval from a minimum of two (2) designated Council members.
  • Article 13.2: Individuals holding membership on the Council are expressly prohibited from initiating or establishing a colony.
  • Article 13.3: The individual recipient of the RSC shall assume the position of Governor of the Colony, retaining this office until such time as they voluntarily relinquish their gubernatorial authority.
  • Article 13.4: The Governor shall maintain direct reporting accountability to the Council. A solitary Council member may be assigned to a colony solely for the purpose of supervision. Consequently, direct posting or communication on designated "slides" by any Council member is strictly forbidden.
  • Article 13.5: Citizens residing within the Colonies shall possess the franchise to participate in elections for Council membership. The official electoral instrument, a Google Document, shall be furnished to the Governor, who shall retain the discretion to determine whether or not such citizens shall be permitted to vote.
  • Article 13.6: Individuals currently serving as government officials within any other designated "slideshow" are ineligible for appointment or service within a colony.
  • Article 1S.7: The designation and naming of a colony shall be the prerogative of the Council, following the submission of a suggestion by the Governor.
